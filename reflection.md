1. What I Got Wrong at the Start
- Initially, I assumed local shops might not have the latest clothing, trending fashion, shoes, or newly launched electronics.
- Field conversations changed that assumption: local shops do stock newer and trending products.
- The bigger issue is online visibility, not necessarily product availability.
- Instagram and Facebook promotions exist, but they do not guarantee that content reaches the relevant nearby customers.
2. What I Would Cut
- Inventory management.
- Stock quantity tracking.
- Product count management.
- My reasoning: the initial problem is product discovery and visibility. Inventory management can be considered later; shopkeepers can manage stock manually during the initial stage.
3. Where AI Helped
- I divided LocalLens development into multiple stages.
- I used prompts to explain each stage and get help implementing code and functionality.
- AI helped accelerate implementation according to my planned development phases.
4. Where AI Wasted Time
- AI sometimes went beyond the initial MVP requirements.
- It explored or suggested features such as inventory management that were not necessary for solving the primary problem.
- This created unnecessary implementation and debugging work.
- My learning: keep AI focused on the actual problem and current development phase.
